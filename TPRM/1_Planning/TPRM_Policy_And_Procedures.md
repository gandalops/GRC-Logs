# TPRM Policy & Procedures

## Why This Policy Exists

Without a documented policy, TPRM ends up inconsistent — different teams 
handling vendor risk differently, no clear ownership, and no enforceable 
gate stopping a risky contract from being signed. This policy exists to 
make third-party risk management consistent, enforceable, and 
risk-based across the organization.

## Policy vs. Procedure

- **Policy** = the rules and why they exist
- **Procedure** = the actual steps to follow to apply those rules

## Key Definitions

- **Third Party** — any external entity the organization has a business 
  relationship with (vendors, suppliers, service providers, consultants)
- **TPRM** — the process of identifying, assessing, mitigating, and 
  monitoring risk arising from third-party relationships
- **Risk** — the potential for a threat to exploit a vulnerability and 
  cause harm
- **Threat** — a potential cause of harm
- **Vulnerability** — a weakness that could be exploited
- **Control** — a safeguard put in place to reduce risk
- **Risk Appetite** — how much risk the organization is willing to accept

## Scope

**In scope** — vendor categories including:
- Infrastructure providers
- Platform providers (PaaS)
- Software application providers (SaaS)
- Managed Service Providers (MSPs)
- Financial/payroll processing
- Data hosting providers
- Telecommunications providers

**Out of scope** — reputational risk and ESG risk are handled by other 
functions; this policy focuses specifically on **enterprise/operational 
risk** from third parties.

## Roles & Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Business Owner / Requester | Initiates the vendor request, owns the 
business relationship |
| TPRM Analyst / Case Manager | Manages the case end-to-end, coordinates 
the assessment process |
| Technical Subject Matter Expert | Evaluates technical/security controls |
| Legal Subject Matter Expert | Reviews contract terms and legal risk |
| Procurement / Buyer | Manages sourcing and commercial terms |
| Legal Team | Drafts and negotiates contract language |
| Risk Committee | Approves high-risk findings and exceptions |
| Single Point of Contact (SPOC) | One designated contact per vendor 
engagement, to keep communication and accountability clear |

## The TPRM Lifecycle

**1. Due Diligence**
The vendor is assessed before any contract is signed. This includes 
risk tiering (scoring the vendor's inherent risk based on data access, 
criticality, etc.) and applying relevant controls. Whatever risk 
remains after controls are applied is the **residual risk** — this must 
be documented, along with who is accountable for accepting it.

**2. Contracting**
Once due diligence is complete, the relationship moves into contracting. 
This typically starts with a **Master Service Agreement (MSA)**, with 
individual engagements covered under **Local/Statement of Work 
Agreements**. Amendments happen for things like payment changes or 
scope/service changes; a **new contract** is required when the change 
is significant enough to fall outside the original agreement's scope.

**3. Continuous Monitoring**
Monitoring frequency is based on vendor criticality:
- **Low-risk vendors** — annual reassessment
- **High-risk vendors** — quarterly reassessment

**4. Disengagement**
When a contract ends, offboarding needs to happen immediately — access 
revocation, data return/deletion, and closing out the relationship 
cleanly, not left open-ended.

**5. Continuous Improvement**
The program itself needs to evolve — refining tools, updating policy 
for emerging risks, benchmarking against industry standards, and 
maturing the program's overall capability over time.

## Regulatory & Compliance Considerations

Depending on the vendor and data involved, relevant regulations may 
include HIPAA, PCI DSS, GDPR, DORA, and alignment with frameworks like 
NIST.
