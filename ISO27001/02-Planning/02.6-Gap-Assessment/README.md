## 1. Clauses 4–10 Assessment Execution Framework

The execution of an ISMS Gap Assessment requires systematically evaluating existing business practices against ISO/IEC 27001 Clauses 4 through 10. The assessment follows a 4-step execution workflow:

```text
  [1. Document Discovery] ──► [2. Stakeholder Interviews] ──► [3. Evidence Verification] ──► [4. Gap Scoring & Plan]

```

1. **Document Discovery:** Review existing organizational policies, organizational charts, process maps, and technical specifications.
2. **Stakeholder Interviews:** Interview key leaders across Legal, HR, IT, Engineering, and Operations to evaluate real-world practices.
3. **Evidence Verification:** Validate verbal assertions against timestamped, unedited records.
4. **Gap Scoring & Plan:** Assign 3-tier compliance ratings (Compliant, Partially Compliant, Non-Compliant) and formulate remediation tasks.

## 2. Mandatory Evidence Checklist (Clauses 4–10)

When scoring each management system clause, evaluate compliance strictly against the existence and operational validity of specific primary evidence artifacts:

| ISO Standard Clause | Clause Title | Primary Evidence Required for "Compliant" Rating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Clause 4.1** | Understanding the Organization | Context Analysis Register listing internal/external issues and business drivers. |
| **Clause 4.2** | Interested Parties | Interested Parties Matrix defining statutory, regulatory, and contractual obligations. |
| **Clause 4.3** | ISMS Scope | Signed ISMS Scope Statement defining logical, physical, and operational boundaries. |
| **Clause 5.1 & 5.2** | Leadership & Policy | Approved Information Security Policy signed by Executive Leadership; resource commitment logs. |
| **Clause 5.3** | Roles & Responsibilities | Approved Security RACI Matrix and formal job descriptions with assigned ISMS duties. |
| **Clause 6.1.2** | Risk Assessment | Documented Information Security Risk Assessment Methodology with calibrated impact/likelihood scales. |
| **Clause 6.1.3** | Risk Treatment & SoA | Populated Information Risk Register, Risk Treatment Plan (RTP), and approved Statement of Applicability. |
| **Clause 6.2** | Objectives & Planning | Documented, measurable Information Security Objectives with assigned tracking KPIs and owners. |
| **Clause 7.2 & 7.3** | Competence & Awareness | Employee security awareness training logs, onboarding records, and role competency evaluations. |
| **Clause 7.5** | Documented Information | Document Control Procedure detailing creation, approval, versioning, and retention rules. |
| **Clause 8.1** | Operational Control | Operational process documentation and execution logs verifying risks are managed as planned. |
| **Clause 9.1** | Monitoring & Evaluation | Security metric reports, KPI tracking dashboards, and vulnerability/incident evaluation logs. |
| **Clause 9.2** | Internal Audit | Independent Internal Audit Charter, Audit Schedule, Formal Audit Report, and finding logs. |
| **Clause 9.3** | Management Review | Formal Management Review Meeting Minutes with executive sign-off and action items. |
| **Clause 10.1 & 10.2** | CAPA & Improvement | Corrective Action Tracker (CAPA) with root cause analyses, remediation deadlines, and re-test logs. |

## 3. Gap Prioritization & Sequencing Rules

Once gaps are identified across Clauses 4–10, apply the following strict sequencing rules to allocate budget and engineering resources effectively:

```text
  [Priority 1: Structural Non-Compliances] ──► [Priority 2: Partial Gaps] ──► [Priority 3: Optimization]

```

* **Priority 1: Structural Non-Compliances (Clauses 4, 5, 6):** Remediate missing foundational requirements first (Context, Scope, Leadership, Risk Methodology). Attempting to fix operational clauses without foundational governance invalidates down-stream work.
* **Priority 2: Execution Gaps (Clauses 7, 8):** Address missing operational mechanisms, awareness programs, and documented procedures once governance boundaries are active.
* **Priority 3: Formalization of Partial Gaps (Clauses 9, 10):** Refine existing informal practices, establish evidence logging mechanisms, and formalize review cadences prior to internal audit execution.

## 4. Gap Assessment Failure Modes

| Failure Mode | Root Cause | Systemic Consequence | Remediation Strategy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Early Annex A Assessment** | Scoring all 93 Annex A controls before establishing the management system or risk register. | Wastes weeks on unverified control selection and generates a bloated, unmanageable baseline report. | Restrict initial gap assessment strictly to Clauses 4–10; postpone Annex A evaluation until Risk Treatment. |
| **Opinion-Based Rating** | Assigning "Compliant" status based on verbal assurances without checking tangible evidence. | Creates false security confidence; gaps are exposed publicly during Stage 1 certification audits. | Enforce an absolute rule: **No Artifact = Non-Compliant**. |
| **Rating Inflation** | Softening gap severity to avoid uncomfortable executive conversations or present a false readiness picture. | Deprives the program of necessary funding/headcount and guarantees audit failures later. | Report raw, unvarnished compliance metrics; present gaps as clear financial and operational funding requests. |
| **Confusing Gap Assessment with Internal Audit** | Treating the initial gap assessment as the formal Clause 9.2 Internal Audit requirement. | Leaves Clause 9.2 unfulfilled, resulting in an automatic major non-conformity during Stage 1/Stage 2 audits. | Maintain clear distinction: Gap Assessment baselines design at launch; Internal Audit checks operational execution later. |
