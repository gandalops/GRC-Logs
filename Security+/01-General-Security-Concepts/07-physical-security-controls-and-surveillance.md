# 1.6 General Security Concepts: Physical Security Controls & Surveillance

## Overview

Physical security encompasses tangible measures, administrative personnel, and electronic surveillance designed to protect physical sites, assets, and personnel from unauthorized access, physical threats, and environmental hazards. Modern physical security employs a defense-in-depth model combining physical barriers, access vestibules, monitoring technologies, and detection sensors.

---

## 1. Physical Barriers & Access Control Structures

Physical access controls manage entry points, restrict unauthorized foot/vehicle traffic, and enforce identity verification:

| Control Type | Operational Mechanism | Primary Purpose | Implementation Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Barricades & Bollards** | Concrete pillars, water hazards, or heavy barriers | Prevents vehicle ramming and channels pedestrian movement | Concrete bollards outside entrances; perimeter moats/bridges |
| **Access Control Vestibules** | Multi-door enclosure ("mantrap") with interlocking doors | Controls ingress, prevents tailgating, and isolates unauthorized personnel | Dual-door badge/biometric reader rooms in data centers |
| **Fencing & Wire** | High perimeter fencing (opaque or transparent) with top deterrents | Defines physical boundaries and deters/delays climbing or breach | High chain-link fencing with razor wire across top edges |
| **Security Guards & Badging** | On-site personnel validating IDs and visible photo badges | Validates human identity; enforces two-person integrity | Lanyard photo badges with embedded RFID; guard front desks |

---

## 2. Surveillance, Lighting & Motion Detection Technologies

Monitoring and detection controls provide continuous visibility and threat detection across facility zones:

| Security Technology | Operating Principle | Advantages | Operational Use Case |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **CCTV & Intelligent Analytics** | Networked cameras sending video feeds to central storage | Built-in motion, facial recognition, and license plate detection | Monitoring loading docks, parking lots, and building entry points |
| **Facility Lighting** | High-intensity illumination of pathways, doors, and perimeters | Eliminates dark zones; improves facial recognition camera capture | 24/7 parking lot lighting angled toward entry cameras |
| **Infrared (IR) & Thermal** | Emits/detects infrared radiation to identify motion or heat signatures | Operates effectively in total darkness; low light capabilities | Dark corridor motion sensing and night-vision cameras |
| **Microwave Sensors** | Emits high-frequency radio waves to detect movement over large areas | Covers significantly broader open areas compared to IR | Open perimeter monitoring and warehouse floor coverage |
| **Ultrasonic & Pressure** | Sound wave reflection detection; force change sensing | High accuracy for localized proximity and collision detection | Under-carpet pressure pads; loading dock collision avoidance |

## 3. Industry Framework Cross-References

To contextualize physical security and surveillance within recognized global standards:

* **NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5:**
  * *Physical and Environmental Protection (PE-2, PE-3):* Physical Access Authorizations and Access Control
  * *Physical and Environmental Protection (PE-4):* Access Control for Transmission Mediums
  * *Physical and Environmental Protection (PE-6):* Monitoring Physical Access
* **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 / 27002:2022 Annex A:**
  * *Control 7.1 (Physical security perimeters):* Protects areas containing information and assets
  * *Control 7.2 (Physical entry):* Restricts physical access through locks, vestibules, and reception desks
  * *Control 7.4 (Physical security monitoring):* Implements surveillance (CCTV) and alarm systems
* **CIS Critical Security Controls v8:**
  * *Control 1 (Inventory and Control of Enterprise Assets):* Maintains physical perimeter visibility over asset locations
  * *Control 3 (Data Protection):* Secures physical infrastructure holding sensitive storage arrays and systems
